Yes. On a wood-framed deck, flashing tape (also called joist tape) is one of the cheapest ways to add years to your structure. We've built roughly 500 decks over 20+ years, and every wood frame we build today gets taped.

It's also one of the most argued-about topics in deck building. Every time we post a video of us taping a frame, the comments fill up with "pros" and DIYers calling it a waste of money. Here's what we've actually seen, which tapes we've tried, and how we install it.

## Why wood deck frames rot from the top down

Wood that gets wet and then sees sun and airflow dries right back out. The problem is the top of your joists and beams. Once deck boards go on, water that gets between the board and the framing has nowhere to go and no sun to dry it. It just sits there, and so does every screw hole that lets it soak deeper.

It doesn't matter how dry your climate is. Moisture trapped on top of wood with no sunlight will eventually rot it out.

### The re-deck that taught us the hard way

Early on, we took a job in Pleasant Grove at the mouth of the canyon. It was supposed to be a simple re-deck: pull off the old wood boards and install new composite. Once the boards came off, the joists were practically falling apart.

We had nothing in the contract covering hidden damage, so we paid to replace the joists out of pocket. They were 20 feet long, so they weren't cheap. Two lessons came out of that job:

- Most old wood frames we uncover have at least a few joists that are completely rotted.
- That's why we no longer take re-deck jobs. Putting new boards on an old, untaped frame is putting a 25-year surface on a structure that may not last five.

### What we found after a heavy-snow winter

After one especially hard winter, we were called out to several high-elevation cabins where heavy snow had collapsed decks. Every deck we inspected had bad rot in the posts, beams, joists, ledgers and old deck boards.

Most of them were older decks. Years of freeze-thaw cycles had weakened the wood, and when a big snow load finally came, they gave out.

## The different types of deck flashing tape

Joist tapes differ in two main ways: the adhesive and whether they have a release-paper backing. Widths range from narrow rolls for joists up to wide rolls for beams and fascia seams.

| Type | What it is | Our take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acrylic adhesive | Pressure-sensitive acrylic glue on a film backing | Our preference. Sticks better and holds up when you lay it in extreme heat without tearing or getting gooey. |
| Butyl adhesive | Thick, rubbery butyl glue | Works fine. Softer in high heat. In our experience, both butyl and acrylic seal around screws, whatever the marketing says. |
| No paper backing | Rolls on and sticks right away | Our go-to for joists. Faster, and no paper strips blowing around the yard on windy days. |
| Paper backed | Peel the release paper as you go | Best for wide rolls (4", 6", 10"). It won't stick to itself or anything else until you're ready. |

We've installed G-Tape, Trex Protect, Zip, Camo, DeckWise, Polyguard and a few others. We even made a reel where our employee Nate compared several brands side by side for ease of installation. He picked the same winner we did.

The best tape is a reputable brand installed correctly. Read reviews, ask around, and pick one you can get consistently.

## Where and how we install it

Our rule: tape every horizontal surface where water can sit. In order:

1. **Post tops and beams first.** Any flat surface that will hold moisture gets covered.
2. **Drop beams before joists.** When the joists cantilever over a beam set low, we tape the beam before a single joist goes on. Then we mark joist layout on the tape with a silver Sharpie.
3. **Joist tops.** Every joist, full length, after the frame is set.
4. **Sandwiched seams.** Anywhere two boards are fastened face to face, like the fascia over the rim board, gets covered with wide tape so water can't wick into the gap.
5. **Picture-frame blocking.** Wide paper-backed tape makes the border framing quick to cover.
6. **Stairs.** We tape the stringers on both the tread and riser cuts.

The deck screws go right through the tape. That's fine, since the tape seals around each fastener.

## What it costs

On a typical 400 sq ft deck, tape adds about $300 to $400 in material and 3 to 4 hours of labor. Compare that to replacing rotted 20-foot joists, or the whole deck, years early.

We don't list it as a separate line item. It's built into our framing price, and our proposals call it out as included. Most homeowners don't think about it until they see us rolling it on. Several have come out to ask what it is, and more than a few have thanked us for doing it.

## When tape isn't enough

Tape protects the top of the frame. It can't fix a frame that sits in a wet, airless spot.

- **Ground-level decks.** When a deck sits right on or near the ground, we tell homeowners that even treated lumber with tape on top won't give them a long-lasting wood frame. For these, we recommend composite framing like [OC Lumber](/oc-lumber).
- **Hot tubs, outdoor kitchens and long spans.** These are where we steer people toward Fortress steel framing.
- **Existing frames.** Tape on an old frame can't undo rot that's already there.

To be fair, joist tape is still fairly new. We haven't seen it fail, but nobody has 30 years of data yet. That's why we started our own test.

## Our long-term test deck

A few months ago we built a small 2' x 2' test deck in our yard, set on 4x4 posts about 18" off the ground. It's split down the middle so each variable has a control:

- **Joist tops:** G-Tape on one half, bare wood on the other.
- **Fascia seam:** taped on one side, untaped on the other.
- **Framing lumber:** two different types, one per half.
- **Decking:** TimberTech Legacy (wood-fiber composite) and TimberTech Landmark (PVC).
- **Edges:** a glued, pocket-screwed miter on one side and a glued-on skin piece on the other.

Both boards are fastened with standard countersunk deck screws driven right through the tape. We'll pull it apart and report back at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 5 years and 10 years. Follow us on Instagram to see each check-in.

## The bottom line: what's the harm?

Most reputable deck builders we know agree tape is worth it on wood framing. It costs little compared to what you're already spending, and if it buys your deck another 2 to 10 years, why wouldn't you?

**If you're getting bids from contractors:**

- Ask whether joist tape is included.
- If it isn't, ask them to add it.
- Or buy it and tape the frame yourself before the boards go down.
- If they refuse, find another builder.

**If you're building it yourself:**

- Pick a reputable brand with solid reviews.
- Tape every horizontal surface, not just the joists.
- Tape low beams before the joists go on top.
- Ask questions in groups like Deck Building 101 on Facebook.

This applies in any climate. And when someone in the comments says it's a waste of money? Ignore the haters.

## FAQ

**Is flashing tape required by code?** Not typically. It's an added layer of protection, not a substitute for proper framing.

**Does it work on treated lumber?** Yes. Treated lumber still rots when water sits on it with no airflow.

**Can I add tape to my existing deck?** Only if you remove the deck boards. If you do, inspect the joists first, since rot is often already there.
