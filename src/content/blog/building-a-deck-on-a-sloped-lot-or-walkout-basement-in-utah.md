**Short answer:** a sloped yard or a walkout basement is one of the best spots for a deck, because you get a second-story deck with a view on top and a shaded outdoor room underneath. It just takes more planning than a deck on flat ground. The frame, the footings, the stairs and the space below all need to be designed together.

A lot of the homes we build for in Utah County and Salt Lake County sit on a slope. Traverse Mountain in Lehi, the Pleasant Grove foothills, Eagle Mountain, Highland and Draper are full of walkout basements where the back door is 10 or more feet above the yard. Here's how we approach those decks.

## Tall decks need a stiffer frame

The taller a deck is, the more any flex or movement in the frame shows up. Long wood posts and beams can twist and shrink as they dry, and over time that shows up as bouncy floors, wavy boards and loose railings. On a tall deck there's also no hiding it.

That's why we recommend [steel deck framing](/steel-deck-framing) for most tall decks. Fortress Evolution steel joists and beams are straight, they don't rot, shrink or twist, and they can span farther than wood. That means fewer posts in your patio below and a flatter, quieter deck on top. Our post on [Fortress Evolution steel framing](/blog/steel-deck-framing-fortress-evolution) covers the details. Where steel isn't the right fit, [OC Lumber](/oc-lumber) Glas-Powered Lumber is another rot-proof option.

## Footings on a hillside

On a slope, every footing sits at a different height, and the soil can be anything from rocky fill to Utah County clay. Getting the footings right is what keeps a tall deck level for decades.

On many hillside decks we use American Ground Screw footings instead of poured concrete. They're driven to the depth and resistance the deck needs, they're ready to build on the same day, and there's less digging on a steep or tight lot. Read more in our post on [ground screw footings](/blog/rethinking-deck-foundations-ground-screws-the-modern-alternative-to-concrete-footings).

## Plan the stairs early

Going from a second story down to the yard takes a lot of steps, and the stairs end up being one of the biggest design decisions on the deck. Things we work out up front:

- **Where they land.** Next to the patio, toward the lawn, or toward a side gate.
- **Straight run or landing.** A long straight stair is simpler; a landing breaks it up, can turn a corner and gives you a place to rest.
- **Lighting.** Riser lights or post-cap lights on every step make a long stair safe at night.

On a second-story deck in [American Fork](/service-areas/american-fork), we ran one long straight stair with a light in every riser, so it's easy to use after dark and looks great from the yard.

## Railing that keeps the view

Walkout lots usually come with a view, and the railing is right in your line of sight. Black aluminum railing with thin balusters, cable railing, or frameless glass all keep the view open. On a covered deck in the [Pleasant Grove](/service-areas/pleasant-grove) foothills, IG frameless glass railing lets you see straight out over Utah Valley, and its lit top edge glows at night.

![Illuminated IG glass railing on a covered second-story deck overlooking Utah Valley city lights](/images/projects/pleasant-grove-foothills-french-white-oak/illuminated-glass-railing-utah-valley-city-lights-1600.webp)

## Make the space underneath usable

The space under a second-story deck is free square footage. Without a plan it ends up as a dark, drippy spot for the garbage cans. With one, it becomes a shaded patio, a hot tub spot, or covered storage.

- **Keep it dry.** An under-deck drainage system like [Trex RainEscape](/blog/trex-deck-drainage-systems-installed-by-tc-decks) catches the water that falls between the deck boards and carries it to the edge, so the patio below stays dry.
- **Finish the ceiling.** A clean ceiling with recessed lights and a fan turns it into a real outdoor room.
- **Use it.** Many clients put a seating area, an outdoor TV or a [hot tub](/hot-tub-decks) under the deck, where it stays out of the sun and snow.

On an [Eagle Mountain](/service-areas/eagle-mountain) home, the second-story deck holds a full dining set looking out over the valley, and the patio underneath stays shaded all afternoon.

![Second-story composite deck with black railing and a shaded patio below in Eagle Mountain, Utah](/images/projects/eagle-mountain-second-story/second-story-deck-patio-below-1600.webp)

## Add a roof for two outdoor rooms

If you want shade up top as well, a [covered deck](/covered-decks) on a walkout gives you two rooms in one build: a covered deck off the kitchen and a dry patio below. It's one of the most popular setups we build on new homes along the Wasatch Front.
