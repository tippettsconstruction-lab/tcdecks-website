**Short answer:** yes, any deck board gets hot in direct Utah sun in July, and composite can get hotter than wood. But you have a lot of control over it. Board type, color, shade and which way the deck faces make a big difference, and the right mix gives you a deck you can walk on barefoot most of the summer.

It's one of the most common questions we get on estimate calls, especially from families with kids and dogs. Here's what actually matters.

## Why decks get hot here

Utah has high elevation, dry air and a lot of clear-sky days, so the sun is strong. A west-facing deck in Saratoga Springs, Lehi or Eagle Mountain takes the full afternoon sun right when people want to use it. Any solid surface in that sun will heat up: wood, composite, PVC, concrete or stone.

Composite boards are dense, and the dark colors absorb a lot of heat. That's why some people end up with a deck they can't walk on at 4 p.m.

## 1. Pick the right board

Not all synthetic boards are the same.

- **Traditional capped composite** is a wood-and-plastic core with a protective cap. It's a great value, but it tends to hold the most heat.
- **TimberTech Advanced PVC** has no wood in it at all. It's lighter and stays noticeably cooler underfoot than traditional composite in the same color. That's a big reason we lead with TimberTech on sunny decks.

We go into the trade-offs in more detail in [is higher-end PVC decking worth it?](/blog/is-higher-end-pvc-decking-worth-it) and on our [TimberTech deck page](/timbertech-decks).

## 2. Go lighter on color

Color matters as much as material. A light gray, white oak or light tan board reflects more sun than a dark walnut or espresso board. If your deck faces west or south and won't be covered, a lighter field color is the single easiest fix.

You don't have to give up dark colors completely. A [two-tone deck](/blog/two-tone-deck-ideas-picture-frame-borders-and-breaker-boards) with a light field and a darker border or stair nosing gives you the contrast without a dark surface under your feet.

## 3. Add shade

Shade beats everything else. A covered deck stays comfortable even on the hottest afternoons, and it protects your furniture and the boards from UV too. Your options include:

- **A full roof** tied into the house, with a ceiling, lights and a fan.
- **A louvered roof** like Sundance, which opens for sun and closes for shade or rain.
- **A pergola**, which filters the sun and can take shade cloth or a polycarbonate top.
- **Shade sails**, a lighter option for part of the deck.

The Highland deck at the top of this post is TimberTech Legacy Pecan, a warm mid-tone board, under a vaulted gable roof on cedar beams and stone columns. Under that roof the color choice barely matters for heat. See the styles we build on our [covered decks page](/covered-decks) and our [pergola page](/pergolas).

## 4. Think about direction

Before you pick colors, look at which way the deck faces:

- **East-facing:** morning sun, shade in the afternoon. Almost any color works.
- **North-facing:** the coolest. Dark colors are fine.
- **South-facing:** sun most of the day. Go lighter or add shade.
- **West-facing:** full afternoon and evening sun. Lighter boards plus shade over at least part of the deck.

A second-story deck also shades the patio underneath it, so a lot of our clients end up with a sunny deck up top and a cool lounge area below.

## 5. Small things that help

- Outdoor rugs in the main walking path.
- A water feature, misters or a hot tub nearby for a quick cool-down.
- Planters and privacy screens that cast afternoon shade.
- Light-colored railing caps if people lean on the rail.

## What about pets?

Dogs feel deck heat before you do. If you have pets, a lighter board and a shaded area they can retreat to are worth planning in from the start. A covered section with a ceiling fan is the gold standard.
