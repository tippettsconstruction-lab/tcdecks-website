*By Levi Tippetts, TC Decks, Saratoga Springs, Utah*

Every year I hear the same question from other builders: are trade shows actually worth it, or just a few expensive days away from the jobsite?

My answer: **100% worth it.** I've attended Deck Expo (now part of the Pool | Spa | Patio & Deck Expo) every year for the last six years and the International Builders' Show (IBS) for the last four or five. This year I also worked a show as a partner exhibitor with Owens Corning Lumber. Here's what those shows have done for my business, what they cost, and how to get the most out of one.

## What I've Gotten Out of Trade Shows

### 1. Products that changed how I build

Some of my best business decisions started on a show floor:

- **American Ground Screw footings.** Digging and pouring concrete footings used to be the worst part of every deck. Our soil is hard to dig, then you mix, pour and wait for concrete to cure. [Ground screws](/blog/rethinking-deck-foundations-ground-screws-the-modern-alternative-to-concrete-footings) usually go in under an hour, and we can start framing right away. The screws and install equipment were a heavy upfront investment, but they're one of the best investments I've made in my company. Homeowners love them too: no dirt piles, no concrete mess, a much less intrusive install, and far less worry about hitting sprinkler or power lines.
- **CAMO hidden deck fasteners.** A friend I met at my first show introduced me to the CAMO team and showed me how to use their system. It cut my board install time by about a third, and they gave me an install tool for free.
- **The Smart Level.** I bought it at my first Deck Expo and still use it all the time.
- **Railing, framing and more.** There are always railing vendors with new products at these shows. I was also introduced to [Fortress Evolution steel framing](/steel-deck-framing). I'm now a Fortress partner and have landed several jobs referred to me through Fortress.

### 2. Partnerships you can't get any other way

At IBS last year I introduced myself to the Owens Corning Lumber team and spent real time getting to know their product. That conversation led to an invitation to join their **Pro Build Team**. Since then I've also been asked to help introduce and promote new products for FastenMaster, CAMO, TimberTech and others. Relationships like these simply don't happen from behind a laptop.

I believe [OC Lumber's framing system](/oc-lumber) is going to take over the framing market, and I'm the first builder in my region to really promote and display it. I haven't sold a job with it yet because of the cost, but when it catches on, I'll already know it inside and out.

### 3. The network

I started going for the classes. Now I go mostly for the people. At my first show I finally met friends I'd only messaged on social media, and those friendships keep paying off. Builders with big followings refer Utah homeowners to me. I pick up installation tips and new ideas every year. We lift each other up and push each other to do better.

This year my wife and oldest daughter came with me to New Orleans. My wife came to the parties and get-togethers and met a lot of my old friends. She said it seemed like everyone in the deck community knew me. Going to a show feels like a high school reunion once or twice a year with all my deck buddies. You can't put a price on that.

### 4. The education classes

When I first started going, education was the whole reason I went. Both Deck Expo and IBS put on classes, lectures, live demos and product reveals throughout the show, often taught by the people who designed the product. For a newer builder, that's some of the most valuable time you can spend. You'll learn new installation methods, see how products are meant to be installed, and pick up business tips you won't learn on the jobsite.

My advice: buy the education pass for your first few years. Before the show, go through the class schedule and circle every session that applies to the work you do (or want to do). It's a small upgrade for what you get back.

## Do Customers Care?

Honestly, homeowners don't know or care whether their builder goes to trade shows. What they do notice is **product knowledge**. I see and test new products before they hit the market, often before builders who skip shows have even heard of them. Then I can explain to a customer why ground screws or a hidden fastener system is the better choice. That's a real edge over the competition.

## What Does It Cost?

Approximate pass prices (check each show's website for current rates):

- **Pool | Spa | Patio & Deck Expo:** about $75–$400, depending on NADRA membership and education passes. The location rotates; recent shows have been in New Orleans, Dallas and Las Vegas.
- **IBS:** about $80–$825, depending on NAHB membership and education passes. Usually held in Las Vegas or Orlando.
- **JLC Live:** about $35–$385. A smaller show, usually in Rhode Island.

Ways to save:

- **Ask exhibitors for a free pass.** Manufacturers often give them to partner builders.
- **Book flights and hotels early.** The big shows fill up fast.
- **Go to the happy hours.** There are appetizers everywhere, and brand partners often treat their builders to dinner.
- **Exhibit with a partner if you get the chance.** Helping build the OC Lumber booth meant my trip was fully paid for, and I made a decent profit on top of it. It was hard work, but fun hard work. And no matter how much you plan, something always goes sideways, so be ready to adapt.

## Which Show Should You Go To First?

1. **IBS.** Manufacturers put most of their money and their biggest product reveals here. There's always more to see than you have time for.
2. **Deck Expo.** A solid show, though from my perspective it's been shrinking.
3. **JLC Live.** I haven't been yet but plan to go in 2027. I hear it's smaller and more intimate, which makes it great for networking.
4. **Also on my radar: World of Concrete.** I see a lot of manufacturers there on social media.

## How to Work a Show (Learn From My Mistakes)

- Get the education pass for your first few years and take every class that applies to you.
- Make a must-see list of booths and people.
- Walk the floor in a crisscross pattern so you don't miss anything.
- Leave time to ask vendors real questions, and take the brochures.
- Bring your crew, or at least your lead guy, so they can see and test products firsthand.
- Grab the swag: hats, shirts, stickers and tumblers.
- **Don't try to be everywhere at once.** That was my biggest mistake. Slow down and enjoy the moment.

## Who Should Go?

Anyone and everyone in the industry, especially if you're just getting started. The one exception: don't lose a customer or a job to attend. If you really can't get away, follow along on social media. Otherwise, make it a priority.

## The Bottom Line

Trade shows are worth it for three reasons:

1. **Discover** new products and methods that make you a better builder and build your reputation.
2. **Network** with fellow builders and bounce ideas off each other.
3. **Partner** with the brands and products you stand behind.

I come home from every show energized. I've never had one feel like a waste of time.
