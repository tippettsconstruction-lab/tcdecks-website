**Short answer:** the best time to add a deck to a new home is soon after you close and before the backyard landscaping goes in. Plan for shade, privacy and the space under the deck from day one, because new neighborhoods have no mature trees and the houses sit close together.

A big share of our work is decks for brand-new homes in [Saratoga Springs](/service-areas/saratoga-springs), [Eagle Mountain](/service-areas/eagle-mountain), [Lehi](/service-areas/lehi), [Vineyard](/service-areas/vineyard) and [Herriman](/service-areas/herriman). Most of these homes come with a back door, a small landing or a bare patio slab, and a backyard of dirt. Here's how to turn that into the outdoor space you actually want.

## Why the builder's deck usually isn't enough

When a new home includes a deck at all, it's often a basic one: small, pressure-treated wood framing and decking, and a simple railing. It's built to give the back door a safe landing, not to be a place to eat dinner with the family.

If your home came with a walkout basement and a door 10 feet off the ground, you'll want a deck big enough for a table, a grill and a few chairs, with stairs down to the yard. That's a custom deck, and it's worth designing it the way you want from the start instead of living with a landing.

## When to build: before the landscaping

The order matters. If you can, build the deck first and landscape after:

- **Access.** We need to get equipment and materials into the backyard. That's easy over dirt and hard over new sod, sprinkler lines and a finished fence.
- **Grading and drainage.** Your landscaper can grade around the deck's footings and stairs, instead of us working around their work.
- **Lighting and power.** Wiring for deck lights, a hot tub or a ceiling fan is simpler to plan before the yard is finished.

If the yard is already done, that's fine too. We work around existing landscaping all the time. It just takes more care.

Winter is a great time to get this done. Our schedule is less rushed, and the deck is ready by the time your first spring in the new house arrives. See [why winter works for a deck build in Utah](/blog/best-time-to-build-a-deck-in-utah).

## Plan for shade: new neighborhoods have no trees

In a new subdivision there's nothing between your deck and the sun, and many of these yards face west. A deck that looks great in the plans can be too hot to sit on in July.

- Choose a lighter board color, or a TimberTech Advanced PVC line that stays cooler underfoot. Our post on [whether composite decking gets hot](/blog/does-composite-decking-get-hot-in-the-utah-sun) goes into detail.
- Cover part or all of the deck with a roof, a louvered roof or a pergola. See our [covered decks](/covered-decks) and [pergolas](/pergolas) pages.

## Plan for privacy: the neighbors are close

New-construction lots are tight, and a second-story deck can look right into the neighbor's yard (and theirs into yours). Privacy screens, privacy walls and tall planters along one side of the deck make a big difference. They also block afternoon wind, which matters in open areas like Eagle Mountain. Also check your HOA's design guidelines for things like railing and screen colors before you pick.

## Use both levels of a walkout

Most walkout homes have a basement door right under where the deck will go. Plan that space at the same time as the deck:

- Keep it dry with an under-deck drainage system like [Trex RainEscape](/trex-decks).
- Leave room for a patio, a [hot tub](/hot-tub-decks) or a seating area underneath.
- Use [steel framing](/steel-deck-framing) to reduce posts and keep the frame straight and quiet for the long term.

We cover this in more depth in [building a deck on a sloped lot or over a walkout basement](/blog/building-a-deck-on-a-sloped-lot-or-walkout-basement-in-utah).

## Build it to match the house

A new house deserves a deck that looks like it came with it. We pick board and railing colors that match your trim, stone, roof or front door, often with a [two-tone border](/blog/two-tone-deck-ideas-picture-frame-borders-and-breaker-boards).
