A deck is one of the bigger projects most Utah homeowners take on, and the builder you pick matters more than the boards you pick. A well-built deck on a cheap board will outlast a poorly built deck on the best board money can buy. Here's the checklist we'd use if we were hiring a deck builder in Utah County or Salt Lake County ourselves.

## 1. A Utah contractor license you can verify

Any contractor building a deck in Utah should hold a state contractor license. Ask for the number and look it up on the Utah Division of Professional Licensing (DOPL) website. It takes a minute and tells you the license is active and who it belongs to. Ours is #5899795-5501.

## 2. Insurance

Ask for proof of liability insurance and, if the builder has employees, workers' compensation. If someone gets hurt or something gets damaged on your property, you want the builder's insurance to cover it, not yours.

## 3. Manufacturer certifications

TimberTech, Trex, Deckorators and Fortress all run certification programs for installers. A certified installer has been trained on that brand's installation rules, which matters because composite and PVC decking warranties depend on the boards being installed to spec. Some programs also let a certified installer offer labor coverage on top of the material warranty. You can confirm a builder's status on each manufacturer's "find a contractor" locator.

## 4. Reviews that mention real projects

A high star rating is a good start. Read the reviews themselves and look for specifics: the city, the material, how the builder handled a problem, and whether the same person's name keeps coming up. Reviews that only say "great job" tell you less than reviews that describe the project. Check more than one place, such as Google, Facebook and Houzz.

## 5. Photos of their own work in your area

Ask to see projects similar to yours, ideally nearby. A builder who has done second-story decks on walk-out basements in Saratoga Springs, or hillside decks in Draper, has already solved the problems your yard will throw at them. Be wary of portfolios made up entirely of manufacturer stock photos. You can browse [our project showcase](/project-showcase) or [find projects by city](/service-areas).

## 6. What's under the boards

Ask how the deck will be framed and what the footings will be. In Utah's freeze-thaw climate, the frame and footings decide how long the deck lasts. Good answers include footings set below the frost line or engineered ground screws, joist tape on wood framing, and options like [steel framing](/blog/steel-deck-framing-fortress-evolution) or [OC Lumber](/oc-lumber) that won't rot. A builder who only talks about board colors is skipping the part that fails first.

## 7. A written workmanship warranty

Manufacturer warranties cover the materials. A workmanship warranty covers how the deck was built. Get it in writing and know how long it lasts. Ours is 7 years.

## 8. Who will actually run your job

Ask who you'll talk to after you sign, and who will be on site. Some companies hand projects off to rotating subcontractor crews. Others have the owner or a lead carpenter on the job from start to finish. Neither is automatically wrong, but you should know which one you're getting.

## 9. A clear design before building starts

You should know exactly what you're getting before construction begins: the layout, the height, the stairs, the decking color, the railing and any add-ons. A drawing or 3D rendering avoids surprises. Every booked TC Decks project gets a 3D rendering of the deck on your home.

## 10. A detailed, itemized estimate

Compare estimates line by line, not just by the total. Make sure each one covers the same size, the same material line, the same railing and the same footings and framing. A low bid often turns out to be a different deck. If you need to spread the cost out, ask whether the builder offers [financing](/financing).

## Questions to ask on the first call

- What is your Utah contractor license number?
- Are you certified by the decking and railing brands you're recommending?
- How will the deck be framed, and what footings will you use?
- What does your workmanship warranty cover, and for how long?
- Who will be on site day to day?
- Can I see projects like mine in my area?
