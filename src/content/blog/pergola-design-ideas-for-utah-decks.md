Utah summers are bright and dry, and a deck that faces west or south can be too hot to use from noon until sunset. A pergola or shade cover fixes that, and it also gives the deck a sense of being an outdoor room. Here are the pergola and shade designs we build most often along the Wasatch Front, and how to choose between them.

## Classic timber pergolas

A timber pergola is a frame of posts, beams and rafters with an open top. It gives filtered shade that moves through the day, and it frames the view without closing in the deck.

- **Cedar** is a favorite wood for pergolas. It's naturally resistant to rot and insects, and it looks warm next to composite decking. On our [Santaquin pecan deck](/project-showcase#santaquin-pecan-pergola), a cedar pergola sits over TimberTech Pecan boards.
- **Rafter spacing** decides how much shade you get. Tighter rafters, or a second layer of purlins on top, cast more shade.
- **Finish.** We seal exposed wood with Messmer's UV Plus so it holds its color in Utah's strong sun.

## Covered roofs and louvered roofs

When you want real protection from sun, rain and snow, a solid roof is the answer.

- **Wood-framed roofs** tie into the house roof line and can be finished underneath with a soffit, recessed lights and a ceiling fan.
- **Timber with polycarbonate panels** keeps the open-beam look but stops rain while still letting light through.
- **Louvered roofs** from Sundance have aluminum slats you can open for sun or close for shade and rain.
- **Aluminum covers** such as Evolution covers are low-maintenance and come in clean, modern profiles.

See more options on our [roofs and shade design page](/design-ideas/roofs-shade).

## Shade sails

SugarHouse shade sails are tensioned fabric stretched between posts or the house. They're a good choice when you want strong shade over part of a deck without a full roof, and they add a modern shape to the backyard.

## How to choose

A few questions settle most decisions:

1. **Which way does the deck face?** West and south-facing decks need the most shade. A louvered or solid roof makes the biggest difference there.
2. **Do you want to use the deck in the rain or shoulder seasons?** If yes, go with a solid or louvered roof.
3. **Is there a view to protect?** An open pergola or shade sail keeps sight lines open.
4. **How much upkeep do you want?** Aluminum and louvered systems need almost none. Wood looks great but needs a fresh coat of sealer every few years.
5. **Snow load.** Utah winters put real weight on a roof. Any covered structure has to be engineered and built for snow, especially on the benches and up the canyons.

## Build the pergola with the deck

The best time to add a pergola is when the deck is built. The posts can be tied into the deck framing and footings, and the whole thing is designed to look like one piece.
