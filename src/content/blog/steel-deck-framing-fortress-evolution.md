Most people pick their deck boards and railing first. The framing underneath gets less attention, but it decides how long the deck lasts. A composite or PVC deck board can last for decades. A pressure-treated wood frame often can't keep up. That's why we build many of our decks on Fortress Evolution steel framing.

## The problem with wood framing

Pressure-treated lumber is strong and affordable, but it's still wood:

- **It rots.** Water sits on top of joists, especially where boards and joists meet, and the wood slowly breaks down.
- **It warps and twists.** Lumber dries out after it's installed. Joists crown, twist and shrink, and the deck surface stops being flat.
- **It doesn't match the boards.** Premium decking from TimberTech carries a long warranty. A wood frame may need work long before the boards wear out.

We protect wood frames with G-Tape joist tape when a job uses wood, and that helps a lot. But steel takes the problem off the table.

## What Fortress Evolution steel framing is

Fortress Evolution is a framing system of light-gauge steel joists, beams, ledgers and brackets. The steel is protected with a coating designed for outdoor use. Parts are made to exact dimensions and bolt together with engineered brackets.

## Why steel framing makes a better deck

**It's dead flat.** Steel joists are made straight and stay straight. That means a flatter deck surface, cleaner board lines and fewer squeaks.

**It won't rot.** Water can't rot steel, and termites and insects can't eat it.

**Longer spans.** Steel beams and joists can span farther than wood of a similar size. That can mean fewer posts and footings, more open space under a second-story deck, and a cleaner look from the patio below.

**Fire resistance.** Steel doesn't burn, which is worth thinking about around fire pits and outdoor kitchens and in wildfire-prone areas.

**It matches the life of the boards.** A steel frame under TimberTech decking and Fortress railing gives you a deck where every part is built for the long haul.

## Where steel framing shines

- **Second-story decks**, where long spans and a clean underside matter.
- **Hot tub decks**, which need a stiff, strong frame.
- **Decks with a covered patio below**, especially with a [Trex RainEscape](/blog/trex-deck-drainage-systems-installed-by-tc-decks) drainage system or a finished soffit underneath.
- **Hillside lots**, where tall posts and long beams are common.

## Steel on top of good footings

A great frame needs a great base. We often set steel framing on [ground screw footings](/blog/rethinking-deck-foundations-ground-screws-the-modern-alternative-to-concrete-footings), which go in fast and give a measured, solid footing.
